\subsection{Conditional Cross-View Geometry on C-MAPSS}
\label{sec:results_cmapss}

Using the shared-model protocol in Section~\ref{sec:methods:cmapss}, we evaluated
PROP-DIRECT and B-STDPROB on C-MAPSS FD001~\citep{saxena2008cmapss}. The benchmark
provides a public simulated reference for the geometry analysis; its protocol and
scope are defined before the results below.

\subsubsection{Agreement depends on the view pair}

Table~\ref{tab:cmapss_validation} shows a conditional pattern. PROP-DIRECT reduces the distance to the full view by 18.5\% (90\% CI: 7.8--29.3\%) for View 1 and by 28.9\% (18.4--37.9\%) for View 2. For the two reduced views, however, the distance ordering reverses: PROP-DIRECT exhibits a 36.6\% larger cross-view distance than B-STDPROB (90\% CI: $-62.9$ to $-13.1$\%). The shared model therefore produces closer predictive distributions when a reduced view is compared with the full sensor complement, while the two equally sized reduced views remain farther apart under PROP-DIRECT. Without an oracle posterior, this distance ordering cannot identify which method has the more correct geometry.

The single-view MAEs are close across methods. PROP-DIRECT obtains 16.44, 15.95, and 16.25 cycles for the full, View 1, and View 2 conditions, compared with 16.27, 15.42, and 15.73 for B-STDPROB. The reversal in the reduced-view pair is consequently not explained by a large point-accuracy collapse. It is consistent with a more specific sensitivity to which sensors are present, although the benchmark has no oracle posterior with which to identify the mechanism or rank the two geometries by correctness.

This shared-model result is stronger evidence than the earlier specialist comparison because the conditioning pathway is trained across views, but it remains evidence from a simulated benchmark with artificial deletion. It provides a conditional cross-view result that echoes the importance-awareness question raised by the synthetic study; it does not confirm the synthetic boundary or establish performance under naturally occurring sensor loss. The archived protocol and outputs make a future study with domain-informed critical channels and field data possible.
